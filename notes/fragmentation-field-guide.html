Checked against the literature

IFN → fusion: your data, not the mechanism papers

Resolved, not a blocker — recorded here because it's a genuine divergence from the mechanism literature, and that's worth knowing even when it isn't a problem.

Context-dependent, not a labelling error

Own data overrides — Huh-7 shows IFN-driven fusion

Our elongated bin is the 20-05-2024 MAVS KO IFN repeat folder. The mechanism literature found here points the other way in the systems it was studied in:

CCCP → fission · Drp1-dependent, confirmed causally (Drp1-KO restores signalling CCCP suppresses) IFN-β → fission · STAT5 → PGAM5 → Drp1-S622, shown in neurons, N2A, MEFs, mouse brain Type I IFN/IFNAR → fission · via ISG15/OMA1, in macrophages

None of those are Huh-7. Own data here shows IFN driving fusion in this cell line, which the mechanism papers don't rule out — they're neuron/N2A/MEF/macrophage systems, and the one elongation↔IFN link that does exist in the literature is already known to be context/virus-dependent. Treat Huh-7-IFN-fusion as a real, cell-type-specific result, not an error to chase down. CCCP → fragmented stays uncontested either way.

Patterns across all twelve

  1. Every actively-maintained modern tool refuses a fragmented/elongated category. MiNA, MitoGraph, Nellie, and MiA — four separate teams, same design decision, stated outright in three of their own papers. That's a field-level signal that a fixed image-derived threshold doesn't generalize across cell type, stain, and scope — precisely the wall we hit trying to make one h value work across two conditions.
  2. Where a category does exist, it never comes from tuning a segmentation parameter. It comes from a trained classifier on real labeled ground truth (10,662 hand-labeled objects; or genetic-perturbation identity as the label), or from a wet-lab step that removes the ambiguity before imaging even happens. Nobody derives a splitting rule from the image itself and expects it to travel between conditions.
  3. Nobody uses skeleton length alone as the discriminator. The field's default shape descriptors are aspect ratio, form factor, and solidity — skeleton length shows up only as one input among many in a multi-feature classifier, never as a standalone µm cutoff the way our scrapped pipeline used it.
  4. The compact-object skeleton bias we measured ourselves — 66% vs. 78% of major-axis length recovered — isn't a documented problem anywhere in this literature. Plausibly because nobody else leans on raw skeleton pixel count the way we did. Worth writing up rather than only routing around.
  5. The real category system is richer than a binary. Six subtypes in the most rigorous classifier found: small/swollen globules, straight/twisted/branched tubules, loops. CCCP specifically is known to produce donuts, not just short fragments — worth checking against our own images.
  6. The one non-circular calibration precedent: solidity/perimeter thresholds derived from watching real fission/fusion events, not from a static shape judgment. A route to a defensible threshold if live time-lapse imaging is ever on the table.

Survey · Part IV

Known confounds

Two of these were already visible in our own data before we knew to look for them in the literature.

Documented in literature

2D MIP merging artifact

Zhang et al. document >20× parameter shifts from a 10–35% intensity threshold change, and explicitly note that stressed/rounded cells increase artifactual merging in projection — meaning the fragmented condition is more susceptible to this than elongated, independent of real biology. Matches our own frag_00001, the 41%-saturated field that drove every outlier we saw.

Not found in literature

Skeleton-length compact-object bias

General thinning/skeletonization bias is documented, but nothing found ties the under-recovery specifically to object compactness the way we measured it (66% vs. 78% of major-axis length). Likely because nobody else leans on raw skeleton length as a standalone metric. Worth treating as our own finding, not a known artifact to cite.

Documented, unaddressed by us

Missing donut / swollen / punctate bins

Donut formation is mechanistically distinct from Drp1-fission — driven by PTP/K⁺-channel swelling and self-fusion after membrane-potential loss. CCCP is exactly this kind of stimulus. Our binary scheme has no bin for it, and some of what we called "fragmented" may be donuts, not fragments.

For discussion

Open questions

Not recommendations — the things this survey raises that need a decision before any code gets written.

01

Worth its own write-up?

Huh-7 IFN-driven fusion runs opposite to every mechanism paper found (neurons, N2A, MEFs, macrophages). That's either a genuine cell-type-specific finding worth characterizing on its own, or reason to double-check the own-data result once — either way it's a side track from the pipeline rebuild, not a blocker to it.

02

Replace skeleton length as the primary metric

Aspect ratio, form factor, and solidity are what the field actually uses. Do we drop skeleton length to a secondary role, or keep it as one input among several rather than the sole discriminator?

03

Pick a category strategy, not a threshold

Every real categorical tool in this survey used either hand-labeled ground truth + a trained classifier, or a wet-lab step that avoids the segmentation problem entirely. Which of those two — or continuous-only, MiA-style — fits what we actually have access to?

04

Add bins for donut/swollen, or explicitly exclude them

If CCCP produces donuts, a binary fragmented/elongated scheme may be silently misclassifying part of the fragmented population. Worth a visual check on our own images either way.

05

2D vs. 3D, revisited

MitoGraph exists specifically because 2D MIP destroys topology in non-flat cells. We tested this once (3D barely recovered more objects than MIP) — is that finding specific to our stacks, or does it hold generally?
